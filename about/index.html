How we work

A call to our dispatch line reaches a person who asks what is happening, where and how urgent it is. Calls with active water, sewage or no heat are handled first. Everything else is booked for the earliest slot that fits, and same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

The plumber who arrives diagnoses the problem and gives you a price before work begins. If the job changes once a wall is opened or a camera finds something, the price is discussed again before more work is done.

What we say and what we do not

Massachusetts requires licensed plumbers for permitted work, and permits and inspections follow state and town rules. We do not publish customer reviews or star ratings on this site, and we do not claim awards or years in business that we cannot back up. What is on these pages is what you can verify.

If you want to confirm a plumber's license, the state's eLIPSE portal lists license type and status, and the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters can be reached at 617-727-9952.

Why the local facts matter

Our pages quote the town's own documents, such as Randolph's 2024 drinking water report and its water regulations, because plumbing is local. The same pipe fails in different ways in different towns, and the rules about who owns what change at the property line.

If you notice something on this site that is out of date, call and tell us.

How a call to us works

Every call goes to a dispatcher, not a recording. You say what is happening, where you are and whether water is still running. We ask a few short questions, such as where the main shutoff is and whether any water is near the electrical panel, and then we send a plumber. If it is safe to wait, we tell you so and book the earliest visit. If it is not, we tell you what to do while the plumber is on the way.

Emergency calls are answered at any hour, including nights, weekends and holidays. Planned work such as a water heater swap, a new sink or a sump pump install is scheduled around your day. In both cases you get a plain explanation of the problem and the options before any work starts, and the price is agreed before we begin.

We do not use forms or chat bots. A phone call is faster in a plumbing emergency, and it lets you talk to a person who knows the town.

Why local knowledge matters in Randolph

Randolph grew fast. The population went from 9,982 in 1950 to 29,206 by 1975, which means much of the housing was built within one generation and has reached an age where original pipes, water heaters and sewer laterals are wearing out together. When we walk into a house, that history helps us know what to look for behind the wall.

The town's drinking water is moderately soft, at about 39.6 ppm hardness, and comes from the Great Pond reservoirs that Randolph shares with Holbrook. Soft water is easy on fixtures, but it can be more aggressive toward older lead and copper. A town sampling round found one of thirty homes above the lead action level, which is a good reason to know what your service line and interior pipes are made of.

We also know who to call at the town. The Randolph DPW can be reached at 781-961-0940, and the Building Department at 41 S Main St at 781-961-0921. The plumbing inspector works Monday, Wednesday and Thursday, and calls go in before 9am. Knowing that saves homeowners a wasted morning when a permit is needed.

Where we work and what we will not do

Our home base is 603 S Main St in Randolph. From there we cover Randolph and the neighboring towns of Braintree, Stoughton, Holbrook, Avon and Canton. If you are not sure your address is in range, call (508) 389-4045 and ask.

We will not guess. If a problem needs a camera, a pressure test or a second look, we say so. If a repair is not worth the money because the fixture or heater is near the end of its life, we say that too. And if a job falls outside plumbing, such as electrical work or drywall repair after a leak, we tell you which trade to call.
