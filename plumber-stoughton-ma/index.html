Stoughton's housing eras and what is behind the walls

Stoughton was settled in 1713, incorporated in 1726 and grew from farming into a shoemaking center before becoming a commuter town. Sharon, Canton and Avon were all once part of it. The 2010 census counted 10,818 housing units, and the mix runs from colonial-era farmhouses to postwar capes and newer subdivisions.

A house built before 1900 may have galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains. A mid-century house usually has copper. Newer construction has PEX or CPVC. Each failing in its own way, and a leaking pipe in an 1880s house is not the same job as one in a 1998 house.

Pre-1900 houses

Expect galvanized steel that has rusted narrow and cast iron or clay drains with open joints. Repairs often reveal more than the original problem.

Mid-century capes and ranches

Copper lines with thin walls are now old enough for pinhole leaks. See repiping if leaks repeat.

Newer homes

PEX or CPVC, with failures usually at fittings and valves.

Water in Stoughton: wells and purchased supply

The state's source water assessment for Stoughton lists town wells, including the Goddard, Fennel, McNamara, Gurney, Muddy Pond, Pratt Court and Harris Pond wells, and purchased sources from Canton, Easton and Sharon. That report dates to 2003, so the current mix may differ, and Stoughton's Water Department can confirm what is supplied to your street.

The same report notes that some residences within the protection areas may still use septic systems. If your house is on septic, a backup is a different problem from a town sewer clog, and we tell you before we run a cable.
