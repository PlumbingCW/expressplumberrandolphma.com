A sewer camera inspection in Randolph puts a waterproof camera on a flexible rod into your drain and shows exactly what is wrong. It replaces guesswork with a recorded video, and it is the right step before digging, relining or closing on a house.

How a sewer camera inspection works

Open the cleanout

The camera enters the line at the cleanout, or through a pulled toilet if there is none.

Push the camera

A self-leveling camera head moves down the line with lights on and a transmitter on the cable.

Watch and record

The screen shows the pipe interior in real time, and the footage is saved.

Locate the problem

A receiver above ground finds the transmitter, so we know where to dig and how deep.

Explain the findings

We review the video with you and describe the repair options.

What the camera can show

Tree roots

Fine roots along a joint or a mass that has filled the pipe.

Cracks and broken pipe

Longitudinal cracks, broken sections and holes in clay or cast iron.

Bellies and sags

A low spot that holds water and catches debris.

Offsets

Joints that have shifted, leaving a lip that snags paper.

Scale and grease

Buildup that narrows the pipe.

Collapsed sections

A pipe that has crushed. Cables stop here, and a repair is needed.

If the camera shows heavy roots or grease, a hydro jetting visit may follow. If it shows a break, the sewer line repair page explains your options.

When a sewer inspection pays off

Buying a house is the biggest reason. A home inspector often cannot see inside the building sewer, and a failed line can cost thousands to replace after closing. A camera inspection before the sale gives you a real basis for negotiation.

Repeat backups are the second reason. A line that clogs every few months has a cause, and cabling it again does not remove the cause. A camera finds it.

A third reason is a major project over the line, such as a new driveway, patio or addition. Learning the line's condition first avoids breaking it afterward.

Randolph houses of the 1950s and 1960s

Randolph's population grew from 9,982 in 1950 to 29,206 in 1975. Houses built in that stretch often connect to the street with older clay or cast iron lines now 60 to 75 years old, and these are worth inspecting before a major purchase.

Older village houses

Houses near Randolph Center and Main Street may have older clay laterals or connections that were patched over the years.
