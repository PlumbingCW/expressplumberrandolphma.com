Who owns the water service line in Randolph

The Town's water regulations make customers responsible for cleaning, maintaining, repairing or replacing the service from the property line to the building on a public way. On a private way, the customer is responsible for services from the public way. The Town assumes liability within the public way.

So a leak in the street side of the shutoff is a call to Randolph's DPW water line at 781-961-0942, and a leak between the property line and the house is yours. A plumber can tell you which side the leak is on before any digging.

Emergency water service line repair: signs of a buried leak

  • The meter runs with everything off. Run the 15 minute meter test. A moving dial after the main inside the house is shut means the leak is on the buried line.
  • A wet patch or soft ground appears with no rain. Water follows the pipe and surfaces at the weakest point.
  • Water pressure drops through the house. A leak between the street and the meter reduces pressure.
  • You hear running water in a quiet house. A hissing sound near the meter or the foundation can mean a broken service line.
  • The bill rises sharply. A water bill that doubles without a change in habits often points to a hidden line leak.

What the Town requires for service work

Randolph's water regulations call for a minimum 3/4-inch service with at least 60 inches of cover, and copper Type K tubing for the service pipe. Curb stops are ball valves with a cap and tee handle.

Work that touches a public or private main needs a written permit from the Board before anyone uncovers or connects to it. The regulations require 48 hours of written notice to the Water, Sewer and gas departments before work starts, and a service connection cannot be backfilled before it is inspected.

Only a duly licensed Master Drain Layer may connect to a public water system. We coordinate those steps so your replacement is not delayed by paperwork.

Plan on a few days for permits and notice on a planned replacement. A burst on the line is handled as an emergency first, then permitted.

Repair a section or replace the whole line?

A section repair fits when

  • The leak is at a single spot.
  • The pipe is otherwise sound.
  • The line is relatively new.
  • Access is easy.

A full replacement fits when

  • The line is old galvanized or lead.
  • There are several leaks.
  • Pressure is poor even after a repair.
  • The line is under a driveway you plan to dig anyway.

If your house has a galvanized or old lead service, we say so. The repiping page covers interior supply lines.

Water quality and old services in Randolph

Randolph's 2024 drinking water report lists a 90th percentile lead result of 7 ppb, below the 15 ppb action level, with 1 of 30 sampled homes above it. The town advises flushing taps for several minutes after water has sat unused.

A lead service line is a separate question from a leaking one. If you are digging up the line anyway, we replace it with copper that meets the Town's regulation, which removes the issue at the source.
