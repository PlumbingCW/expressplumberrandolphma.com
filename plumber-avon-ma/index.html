Where Avon's homes and its industry meet

Avon covers about 4.6 square miles, and the town reports that roughly 23 percent of its land is used for industry or commerce. The Avon Industrial Park, near Route 24, spans about 500 acres with 127 firms and more than 3,600 jobs, including a large distribution facility on Parker Drive.

That combination shapes the call list. Houses on the residential streets need ordinary repairs. Businesses in the park need after-hours help with restrooms, water heaters and drain lines, which is why our commercial plumbing crew covers Avon too.

Emergency plumbing calls in Avon: what to do first

  • Water pouring from a pipe. Close the main shutoff and open a low tap. Call the line right away.
  • Sewage in a floor drain. Stop all water use. Do not run the washer or flush.
  • No hot water in a business. Check the breaker or gas valve, then call, and tell us the unit type.
  • A basement with standing water. Stay out if outlets or the panel are wet, and call.

Avon's groundwater wells and septic neighbors

The Avon Water Division pumps its water from groundwater wells, and the state's source water assessment lists the Memorial, Porter, Theater, Connolly Road and Troutbrook wells, among others. The assessment, which dates to 2003, notes that the residential areas inside the protection zones use private septic systems, with more than 100 septic systems or cesspools identified.

If your property is on septic, a slow drain or a gurgling toilet may be a septic problem, not a town sewer clog. We tell you which it is before cabling, and septic pumping is a separate trade we refer when needed.

A small town with a long history

Avon separated from Stoughton and was incorporated on February 21, 1888. It was named for Stratford-upon-Avon in England, and its seal carries William Shakespeare. The early settler was a blacksmith from Braintree named Moses Curtis, who built around 1720 in what was called Curtis Corners.

The 2000 census counted 1,740 housing units. A town that small has houses of many ages in a short area, and we often find a 1940s house next to a 1990s house. Each needs its own approach.
