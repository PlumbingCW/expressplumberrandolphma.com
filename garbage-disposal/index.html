Disposal troubleshooting before you call

A few safe checks can save a visit.

  • Turn it off. Switch off the wall switch and, for a hard-wired unit, the breaker, before reaching near it.
  • Check the hex slot. Many disposals have a hex socket on the underside. A hex key turns the flywheel and frees a jam.
  • Press the reset button. A red button on the bottom resets an overheated motor.
  • Look for a leak. Water at the bottom means a failed seal. Water at the top points to the sink flange.

Never put a hand into the disposal, even when it is off. If the unit hums but does not turn, a plumber clears the jam safely.

What a failed disposal is telling you

Humming but not spinning

A jammed flywheel. A hex key or a plumber frees it.

Silent with the switch on

A tripped reset, a dead outlet or a burned motor.

Leaks from the bottom

A cracked housing or a failed internal seal. Replacement is the fix.

Leaks from the drain pipe

Loose fittings or a cracked trap arm. A repair is possible.

Slow drain after grinding

A clogged trap arm, not the disposal itself. See drain cleaning.

Smells bad

Food stuck in the grinding chamber or the drain. A cleaning and a flush help.

What not to grind, and what to do instead

A disposal handles soft food scraps, not grease, bones, coffee grounds in bulk or fibrous vegetables such as celery in large amounts. Grease cools in the drain line and builds up.

In Randolph's older houses, kitchen drains run through small branch lines that clog easily. If the disposal is fine and the sink still drains slowly, the problem is the pipe, and hydro jetting may be the lasting fix.

Cold water when grinding

Run cold water for 15 seconds after the grind so the particles clear the trap.

Installation, wiring and permits

A disposal that plugs in is an appliance swap. A hard-wired unit needs an electrician for the circuit work. We install the unit, the flange and the drain connections, and tell you if the circuit or the outlet is not up to code.

A like-for-like disposal replacement is a minor fixture job. If the sink drain needs rerouting, the work becomes a permitted plumbing change, and the permit must be pulled by a licensed plumber.

The code behind the work

Disposal connections must meet 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, which governs traps and drain connections under the sink. A licensed plumber pulls the permit, and the inspector signs off on the finished job.
