Emergency sump pump repair: what to check in a flooding basement

Stay out of standing water

If water reaches outlets, a panel or appliances, leave the room and switch off power from a dry spot.

Look at the pit

Is water in it? Is the lid on? Is the float lifting freely?

Check the plug and breaker

A tripped GFCI is a very common cause of a dead pump.

Lift the float by hand

A pump that starts when you lift the float has a float problem. A pump that stays silent has a motor or power problem.

Why sump pumps fail

Stuck float switch

The float rubs the pit wall or tangles with the discharge line, and the pump never turns on.

Clogged intake

Gravel, silt and debris block the pump inlet and the motor runs hot.

Failed check valve

Water in the discharge line drains back into the pit, and the pump cycles constantly.

Burnt motor

A pump running dry or running nonstop burns out. Many last about seven to ten years.

Power loss

Storms cause outages, the same time the pit gets the most water. A battery backup solves that.

Frozen or blocked discharge

The outside pipe freezes or fills with debris in winter. Water has nowhere to go.

Primary pump, battery backup or water-powered backup

Battery backup

  • Runs when the power fails.
  • Needs a charged battery and testing every few months.
  • Good for homes with frequent outages.
  • Sits alongside the main pump.

Water-powered backup

  • Needs no battery or electricity.
  • Uses municipal water pressure to run.
  • Not allowed or practical in every town.
  • Requires adequate water pressure.

The code behind the work

Discharge and drain connections for a sump pump follow the standards in 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code. A licensed plumber pulls the permit, and the inspector signs off on the finished job.
