A commercial district, not a subdivision

Crawford Square is a business district. The town's urban revitalization plan describes it as the traditional downtown of Randolph, covering about 92.8 acres and 88 parcels. North Main Street, which is Route 28, runs through it, and the edge follows segments of North, Short, Moulton, Cottage, Pleasant, West, Warren, Highland and South Main Streets, plus Memorial Parkway.

The plan lists restaurants, financial and legal offices, grocery and convenience stores, health services, automotive businesses, municipal buildings and personal services. Only a small number of homes are scattered through it, though upper-floor housing is allowed under certain standards. Civic buildings include the Turner Free Public Library, Stetson Hall and a fire station.

So this page speaks to building owners, managers and tenants, not to a residential neighborhood. For home calls in the rest of town, see our Randolph page.

What the town's plan says about the buildings

The same plan counts 16 buildings from 1800 to 1920, 42 from 1921 to 1970, 12 from 1971 to 2000 and 4 from 2001 to 2018. It reports that about 78.4 percent of structures predate 1971, and that about half were rated fair or poor for condition. Condition in that survey means the building, not its pipes.

For a plumbing call, the lesson is practical. A building with many decades of changes may have mixed piping, extra shutoffs nobody labeled and drains tied in during old renovations. A plumber should map what is there before promising a fix, and we do that on every commercial visit.

The plan also says water and sewer service extends throughout the area, and that downtown utilities were moved underground. Where the public line ends and your building's line begins is a question for the Randolph DPW at 781-961-0940.

Restaurants and food shops

Kitchen drains, floor drains, hot water capacity and sink fixtures. Ask your landlord who is responsible for the grease interceptor, if there is one.

Offices and shops

Restrooms, flush valves, small water heaters and leaks that show up on a ceiling below.

What to do first in a downtown building

  • Water from a ceiling or wall. Close the building's main or the floor's shutoff, move stock and electronics, and call.
  • A backing up floor drain. Stop using fixtures on that line. Do not run a dishwasher or hose into the drain.
  • Hot water gone in a kitchen. Check the breaker or gas valve, then call and tell us the heater type.
  • A restroom out of service. Close the stop valve behind the fixture, post a sign and call.

Surfaces, floods and drains downtown

The plan reports that about 80.1 percent of the district is covered by impervious surfaces, and that off-street parking takes about 35.6 acres, close to 38 percent of it. Rain on that much pavement runs to storm drains quickly, and plumbing inside the buildings is a separate system from those drains.

It also notes that part of the northern area lies within the 500-year floodplain, and a sliver of one parcel on Highland Avenue lies within the 100-year floodplain. If your building is in that area, ask your insurer what it covers. Large work needs a permit from the Building Department at 41 S Main St.
