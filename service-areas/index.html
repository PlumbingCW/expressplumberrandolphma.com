How we decide what counts as the service area

A service area is a promise about response time, so we keep it to towns we can reach quickly from our base at 603 S Main St in Randolph. Braintree, Stoughton, Holbrook, Avon and Canton are all close enough that an emergency call can be answered without a long drive, and we know the main roads between them.

Each town has its own water and sewer arrangements, its own inspectors and its own housing mix. A plumber who works only in one of them learns those details slowly. Working across all six lets us carry what we learn in one town into the next. A sewer lateral habit common in one older neighborhood, for example, is often worth checking in the next.

If your town is not on the list, call anyway. We will tell you honestly whether we can reach you in a useful time, and if we cannot, we will say so rather than promise something we cannot keep.

What to have ready when you call

A few details speed up any call. Tell us your street address and town, what you see or hear, and whether water is still running. If you can reach the main shutoff, close it before we arrive. If water is near an outlet, a panel or an appliance, stay clear and leave the power alone.

For a planned job, have the age and make of the fixture or heater if you know it, and a short list of what you want done. A photo is useful, but a phone call works fine without one.

For anything involving the town's water or sewer connection, the Randolph DPW is at 781-961-0940, with water on 781-961-0942 and sewer on 781-961-0941. We can help you work out whether the problem is on your side of the property line or the town's.

Why neighboring towns are not interchangeable

Holbrook and Randolph draw from the same Great Pond reservoirs, so the water that comes out of the tap is similar, but the sewer systems, the inspectors and the street layouts are separate. A plumber who treats the two as one town will miss permit rules and utility contacts that differ on each side of the line.

Braintree, Stoughton, Avon and Canton each have their own building department and their own rules for permits and inspections. Before we start permitted work in any of them, we check with the local office, so the paperwork goes to the right place the first time.

On our town pages we describe what we have verified about each one, including housing age, water and the local contacts that matter to a homeowner. If something on a page looks out of date, call us and tell us, and we will fix it.
