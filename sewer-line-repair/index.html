Sewer line problem or just a clogged drain?

The two look alike at the fixture. The fix is very different.

Likely a clog

  • One fixture is slow or blocked.
  • A cable clears it and the drain stays clear.
  • The line has never backed up before.
  • Nothing smells at the cleanout.

Likely a damaged sewer

  • Several fixtures back up together.
  • The drain clears and clogs again within weeks.
  • A cable brings back dirt, roots or pipe pieces.
  • The lawn has a soggy patch over the line.

For a plain blockage, drain cleaning or sewer line cleaning is the right first call. If those fail or return, repair is next.

Emergency sewer line repair: what happens on the call

The dispatcher asks where the backup shows, whether a cleanout is accessible and whether the problem is new. A plumber arrives with a cable and camera. The first goal is to relieve the backup so the house is usable, then to see why it happened.

A recorded camera run follows the pipe from the cleanout to the street connection. If it shows a break, we measure the distance, mark the surface and describe the repair options before any digging.

Spot repair

A single cracked or offset section can be dug out and replaced. This is the fastest route when the rest of the line is sound.

Lining

Lining coats the inside of a cracked pipe with a resin sleeve. It avoids a trench, and it fits when the pipe is not collapsed.

Full replacement

A line with breaks along its length is replaced. A trench or a pipe-bursting method may be used, depending on access.

Public side, private side: who owns what in Randolph

The building sewer, the pipe from your house to the connection with the town sewer, is the property owner's responsibility. The town sewer main in the street is public. Randolph's DPW Sewer line is 781-961-0941 for questions about the street connection.

Before any excavation in the street or on a public way, the work needs coordination with the town and a licensed drain layer. We tell you which permits apply and who files them, and the plumbing permit must be pulled by a licensed plumber.

Roots, trees and the Cochato watershed

Randolph is crossed by the Cochato River and Glovers Brook, and older lots near them often hold mature trees whose roots seek out any joint that leaks water vapor. Tree roots entering a clay joint are one of the most common causes of repeat backups here.

A camera shows whether roots are a thin hair along a joint or a mass that has opened the pipe. If it is hair, hydro jetting may be enough for a while. If the joint is broken, cutting the roots without repairing it only buys months.

The code behind the work

Sewer line repairs must meet 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, including slope, pipe material and cleanout rules, and the town inspects the connection. A licensed plumber pulls the permit, and the inspector signs off on the finished job.
