When a clogged drain is an emergency in Randolph

Some clogs can wait for a morning appointment. These cannot.

  • Water is coming up in the lowest drain. A basement floor drain or tub that fills when the washer drains points to the main line, not a single fixture. Stop all water use now.
  • Every fixture is slow at the same time. One slow sink is a branch clog. Slow toilets, tubs and sinks together mean the blockage is downstream of all of them.
  • The smell is sewage, not damp. Sewage smell with standing water needs gloves, ventilation and a quick call, because the wet area has to be dried and cleaned.
  • A guest bathroom is the only toilet in the house. A single toilet that will not flush is urgent in its own right even if nothing is overflowing yet.

If water is already spreading, shut off the washer and dishwasher, keep children and pets out of the wet area, and call. Our sewer line cleaning crews handle the deeper main line blockages that start this way.

How we clear a drain, from the simple tool to the camera

A kitchen sink clogged with grease and a main line choked by roots are different jobs, and treating them the same wastes time. The plumber starts with the cheapest tool that can actually reach the blockage.

For a single sink, tub or lavatory, that is a hand auger or drum machine run through the trap arm. For a laundry stack or a main cleanout, it is a larger cable with a cutting head. If the cable keeps hitting the same spot or brings back chunks of pipe, the next step is a drain camera so the problem is seen instead of guessed at.

Snaking and cabling

Cables break through soft clogs such as hair, paper and food. They make a hole through grease rather than remove it, which is why kitchen lines can clog again within weeks.

When jetting makes more sense

A cable punches a path. Hydro jetting scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water and is the better fix for grease, scale and root hair.

Why drains clog in Randolph homes

Randolph's housing runs from 19th-century farmhouses near the center to the Capes and ranches built after the town grew from 9,982 people in 1950 to 29,206 by 1975. Postwar houses often have a cast iron or clay line running to the street, and both materials develop rough interior surfaces that catch grease and roots.

That is why a drain can clear on Monday and back up again on Friday. If a line has been cabled twice in a year, a sewer camera inspection is worth more than a third cabling, because it shows whether the pipe is cracked, offset or full of root growth.

Randolph's water is soft, with 39.6 ppm of hardness in the town's 2024 report, so mineral scale is rarely the culprit here. Grease, hair, wipes and roots cause most of the calls.

Keep the drain clear after we leave

  • Keep grease and coffee grounds out of kitchen sinks and scrape plates into the trash.
  • Use a hair catcher in every shower and tub drain.
  • Flush only toilet paper. Wipes marked flushable still catch on roots and rough pipe.
  • Run hot water for a minute after washing greasy dishes.
  • Avoid chemical drain openers in an older pipe, because they can sit in a trap and corrode the fixture.

The code behind the work

Drain and sewer work follows the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, which sets standards for vents, traps and cleanouts. A licensed plumber pulls the permit, and the inspector signs off on the finished job.
