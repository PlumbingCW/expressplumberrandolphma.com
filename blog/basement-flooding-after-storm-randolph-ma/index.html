Basement Flooding After a Storm in Randolph, MA: Steps

By the %s team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

Water in the basement after a storm is stressful, and the first hour matters. The right steps limit damage and keep you safe, and the wrong ones, such as walking into water near the electric panel, can be dangerous.

This guide covers what to do first, how to identify the source of the water and what a plumber can and cannot do.

Clear water or sewage: why it matters

Clear groundwater is a water problem. Sewage is a health problem. If the water comes from a floor drain or a toilet or has a sewage smell, assume it is contaminated. Do not wade through it without protective gear, and do not let children or pets near it.

A sewage backup in a storm can come from your own line or from the town sewer when stormwater overloads it. Randolph's DPW Sewer line is 781-961-0941 for street-side reports. After hours, urgent public works problems go through Randolph Police at 781-963-1212. If the backup is in your own line, our sewer line cleaning crews clear it.

Pump and drain checks during a storm

  • Is the sump pump running? Listen for it. A pump that runs but the pit keeps rising may be overloaded.
  • Is the float moving? A stuck float is the most common reason a pump does not start. See sump pump repair.
  • Is the discharge pipe clear? A frozen or blocked outside pipe sends water back.
  • Are the gutters and downspouts clear? Overflowing gutters dump water along the foundation.
  • Is the floor drain open? A blocked floor drain can back up under load.

Why some Randolph basements flood

Randolph sits in the Cochato River valley, with the Blue Hills to the north. The land drains toward the river and its brooks, and basements near low spots can fill when the ground is saturated. Many postwar subdivisions were built with basement floor drains and little or no pump.

The Town's water regulations require that plumbing be arranged to prevent cross-connections, and they define connections between drinking water and drain piping as cross-connections. That is one reason sump discharge piping needs care. We check where yours is allowed to empty.

What a plumber can fix and what needs another trade

A plumber can fix

  • Failed or undersized sump pumps and floor drains.
  • Backed-up sewer and drain lines.
  • Burst or leaking supply pipes.
  • Discharge piping.

Another trade handles

  • Drying and cleanup of wet materials.
  • Electrical repairs after water damage.
  • Foundation cracks and exterior membranes.
  • Mold testing and removal.

We tell you honestly which is which. Our basement waterproofing page covers the plumbing side of keeping a basement dry.

After the water is out

Remove wet items quickly, run fans and a dehumidifier and check for mold in the days that follow. Wet drywall and carpet that stay damp for more than a day or two need professional drying. Photograph everything for your insurer before you move or throw items away.

How to prepare before the next storm

A few steps before a forecast storm reduce the risk. Clear gutters and downspouts, and extend downspouts several feet from the foundation. Test the sump pump and the battery backup. Move valuables off the floor and onto shelves. Know where the main water shutoff and the electrical panel are, and keep a flashlight handy.

If you have a floor drain, pour a gallon of water into it each season so the trap stays full. A dry trap lets sewer gas rise, and a blocked one can back up under load. If your drain has backed up before, have the line cleaned and camera-checked before the wet season.

Keep a list of phone numbers near the panel, including ours, your insurer and your electrician. In an emergency, you do not want to hunt for them.

Who to call in Randolph

If water is coming from the street side, or you suspect the town's sewer is surcharging, call the Randolph DPW at 781-961-0940. The sewer line is 781-961-0941. They can tell you whether others on your street have reported the same thing, which helps you tell a town problem from a private one.

For anything on your own side of the property line, call us. We can pump out, clear the lateral and check whether a backwater valve would help. Take photos of the water line on the wall before you clean up, since your insurer will want them.
