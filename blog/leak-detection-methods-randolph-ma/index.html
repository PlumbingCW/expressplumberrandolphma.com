Leak Detection Methods Plumbers Use in Randolph, MA Homes

By the %s team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

A hidden leak does not announce itself. It shows up as a water bill that crept up, a stain that grows or a sound in a quiet house. Finding it without tearing apart the wall is a matter of method, and plumbers use a short list of tools in a set order.

This guide explains each method, what it can and cannot tell you and the order in which they are used.

Step one: the meter test

The cheapest method is one you can do yourself, and it tells you whether to look at all.

Turn off all water

Taps, dishwasher, washer, ice maker and irrigation.

Record the meter

Note the dial or the low-flow indicator.

Wait 15 minutes

Use no water.

Read it again

Any movement means a leak on your side of the meter.

Close the main and repeat

If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the main valve.

Acoustic listening and thermal imaging

A pressurized pipe with a hole in it makes a hissing or whooshing sound. Acoustic equipment uses a sensitive microphone or ground sensor to hear it through floors and walls, and filters out background noise. The plumber moves along the suspected path and marks where the sound is loudest.

A thermal camera shows temperature differences. A hot water line that leaks warms the material around it, and a cold line chills it. The camera shows these as patterns on a screen. Thermal imaging does not see through walls, but it shows the surface effect of what is behind them.

Moisture meters

These measure the water content of drywall, wood and concrete. They confirm whether a stain is wet now or dry from an old leak.

Why combining tools matters

Each tool has blind spots. Using two or three together gives a reliable answer.

Pressure testing the system

A pressure test isolates a section of piping, adds pressure and watches whether it holds. A heating loop or an irrigation line can be tested this way. A drop in pressure shows a leak in that section, and the test is repeated on smaller pieces to narrow it down.

For a buried service line, a plumber can isolate the line from the house and test it. If the line fails, we use acoustic gear above ground to locate the leak. Our water service line repair page explains the next steps when the leak is on that pipe.

What Randolph's rules mean for a leak on the service line

The Town of Randolph's water regulations make customers responsible for the service from the property line to the building. They also require copper Type K tubing for services, at least 3/4-inch in diameter and with at least 60 inches of cover. A leak on that line is the owner's to repair, and work that uncovers or alters a main or service needs a permit and notice to the Town.

If the leak is on the street side, call Randolph DPW's water line at 781-961-0942. The 2024 drinking water report shows soft water at 39.6 ppm, which means scale is not the main cause of leaks. Corrosion, age and fitting failures are.

What detection can and cannot do

Detection can

  • Narrow a leak to a small area.
  • Confirm whether a stain is current.
  • Save wall and floor from unneeded openings.
  • Document the leak for your insurer.

Detection cannot

  • See through concrete like an X-ray.
  • Fix the leak by itself.
  • Predict leaks that have not started.
  • Replace a repair.

For the full process, see our leak detection page.

Signs worth investigating now

  • A water bill that rises without a reason. Compare several months, and check the meter.
  • A warm spot on a floor. A hot water line may be leaking under a slab.
  • A musty smell or peeling paint. Moisture is escaping a wall.
  • The sound of running water in a quiet house. Listen near the meter and the water heater.
  • A water meter that moves with the taps off. Do the meter test.

When to call

If the meter test shows a leak, or if you see a stain growing, call. A small leak behind a wall can cause mold and rot. We serve Stoughton and Avon as well, as described on the Stoughton and Avon pages. If water is actively coming through a ceiling or floor, shut the main and call the 24/7 line.

What happens after the leak is found

Once the leak is located, the plumber explains the repair. For a pipe in a wall, that often means a small opening, a repair or a replacement section, and a patch. For a slab leak, it can mean breaking a small area of floor or rerouting the line through the walls or ceiling.

A reroute avoids breaking the slab and can be the better long-term answer when the original pipe has failed in multiple places. The plumber describes both options and their trade-offs before work begins.

After the repair, we test the system again to confirm the leak is gone. The meter test is repeated, and the pressure is checked. If walls or floors were opened, the next step is drying and patching, which another trade handles.
