How to Keep Drains From Clogging in Randolph, MA Homes

By the %s team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

A drain does not clog all at once. Grease, hair and paper build up slowly along the pipe wall until one more load of laundry tips it over. The habits that keep a line open are simple and they cost almost nothing.

This guide covers each drain in the house, what to keep out of it and what the older pipes in many Randolph homes need.

Why drains clog in the first place

A drain pipe slopes gently toward the sewer. Water carries waste along, and anything that sticks to the wall stays behind. Over months, layers of grease, soap scum, hair and paper narrow the pipe until a small blockage can stop it entirely.

Pipe condition matters as much as habits. Cast iron develops rough scale on the inside that catches debris. Clay pipe lets roots in at the joints. A pipe with a sag, called a belly, holds water and waste. Habits help every pipe, but a damaged pipe needs repair.

Grease

Hot grease runs freely and then cools and hardens in the pipe. It is the main cause of kitchen clogs.

Hair and soap

Hair knots around the drain stopper and in the trap, bonded together by soap scum.

Monthly habits that keep a line clear

  • Pour hot water down kitchen drains. A kettle of hot water helps move soft grease along, but it does not dissolve a thick layer.
  • Clean the stoppers. Pull the pop-up stopper and clear the hair and gunk from it.
  • Run the disposal with cold water. Cold water keeps grease firm so the disposal can chop it.
  • Flush with water after laundry. A good run of water after a big wash helps move lint.
  • Check the cleanout. Know where your main cleanout is and keep the area clear.

Skip the chemical drain cleaner

Chemical drain openers are tempting, but they are not a good idea in older pipes. They generate heat and can sit in a trap that is not draining, where they can damage fixtures and finishes. They also do not reach a main line blockage and can be dangerous for a plumber working on the line afterward.

A cable or a hydro jet clears the pipe without chemicals. If a drain keeps clogging, a drain cleaning visit with a camera shows the cause, and hydro jetting scours grease and scale from the pipe wall.

Older Randolph houses and their drains

The state survey of Randolph's housing describes a town that grew from 9,982 people in 1950 to 29,206 in 1975, much of it in suburban Capes, bungalows and ranches. Houses of that era usually have cast iron or clay drains that are now 50 to 75 years old.

Randolph's water is soft, so mineral scale from the water is not the issue. Roots, grease and rough pipe interiors are. If you have had two backups in a year, a sewer camera inspection is a better investment than a third cleaning.

What to do when a drain slows down

Plunge first

A plunger clears many sink and tub clogs. Cover the overflow when plunging a sink.

Remove the stopper

Pull the stopper and clean it, then try again.

Try a hand auger

A small hand auger can reach hair clogs in a tub or sink.

Stop if several drains are slow

That points to a main line problem.

Call a plumber

Do not keep running water down a drain that is not clearing.

When prevention is not enough

If every fixture drains slowly, if a floor drain bubbles or if you smell sewage, the blockage is in the main line, and habits cannot fix it. Stop using water and call. Our sewer line cleaning crews clear the main line and run a camera to find the cause.

Teaching the household the rules

Clogs are often a household habit problem more than a pipe problem. One person who pours grease down the sink or flushes wipes can undo everyone else's care. A short rule list on the cabinet door, or a word with kids and guests, does more than any product on a shelf.

Kitchen habits are the easiest to change. Keep a small can by the stove for cooled grease. Scrape plates into the trash before washing. In the bathroom, a hair catcher costs very little and catches the material that causes most tub clogs. In the laundry, a lint filter on the washer's discharge hose is a small purchase that keeps lint out of the line.

If you rent out part of your house or manage a small building, post the rules where tenants will see them. Many main line backups in multi-unit houses trace back to one unit.

A simple monthly routine

Once a month, flush each drain with a kettle of very hot, but not boiling, water. Check the stoppers and strainers and pull out any hair. Run the disposal with cold water for half a minute after use. In the basement, look at the floor drain and the cleanout for any sign of dampness or odor.

A routine like this takes ten minutes and tells you early when something is changing. If one drain gets slower while the others stay fast, the problem is in that branch. If all of them slow down together, the main line is the suspect, and a visit is worth booking before it backs up.
