Kitchen Faucet Hose Repair in Randolph, MA: Pull-Down Fixes

By the %s team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

The pull-down or pull-out hose on a kitchen faucet works hard. It stretches, twists and retracts several times a day, and it is the part of the faucet most likely to leak or stick.

This guide covers the common hose problems, the repairs you can try and the point where a new faucet is the better answer.

How a pull-down faucet works

A pull-down faucet has a spray head on a flexible hose. The hose runs down through the faucet body and under the sink, where it connects to the faucet's water outlet. A small weight on the hose pulls it back when you let go. The hose can be braided nylon or plastic, and it has quick-connect fittings at both ends.

Because the hose passes through the body and under the sink, a problem can be at the spray head, along the hose or at the connection below. Each has a different fix.

Spray head

The head contains a diverter that switches between stream and spray. Scale or debris in the head causes weak flow or leaks at the nozzle.

Hose

A kink, a worn spot or a split causes leaks. The hose can also rub on cabinet items below.

How to replace a kitchen faucet hose

Check the brand, and buy the hose and weight made for your faucet.

Shut off the water

Close both angle stops under the sink.

Clear the cabinet

Remove items, and put a bucket and towel under the connections.

Disconnect the old hose

Release the quick-connect fitting at the faucet's water line.

Pull out the old hose

Draw it up through the faucet, noting how it is routed.

Thread the new hose

Feed it down from the top and connect it below.

Attach the weight

Clip it where the manufacturer says, so the hose retracts smoothly.

Test

Turn on the water and check for leaks at every connection.

When to replace the whole faucet

A hose can be replaced, but if the faucet body is corroded, the cartridge is failing or the finish is pitted, a new faucet may be the better value. A hose and cartridge repair on a faucet that is 15 years old is often followed by another repair soon after.

Our faucet repair team replaces hoses, cartridges and faucets, and can install a customer-supplied faucet. If the supply lines or angle stops under the sink are old, we replace them at the same visit.

Randolph water and faucet parts

Randolph's water is soft, at 39.6 ppm of hardness in the town's 2024 report, so scale clogs aerators and spray heads less than in a hard-water town. A weak spray here is more often debris, a kink or a worn diverter.

The town's report also advises flushing taps for several minutes when water has sat unused, because one of 30 sampled homes was above the lead action level. If you replace old fixtures, running the cold tap for a few minutes afterward clears any debris and is a good habit.

Quick checks before you call

  • Is the hose kinked under the sink? Straighten it and check the weight position.
  • Is the aerator clogged? Unscrew it, rinse it and screw it back.
  • Are the angle stops fully open? A partly closed stop reduces flow.
  • Is there water at the connections? Dry them with a towel and watch for drips.
  • Is the spray head cracked? A cracked head leaks and should be replaced.

When to call a plumber

If there is water under the sink that you cannot trace, if the angle stops will not close or if the faucet body is cracked, call. A faucet that sprays water into the cabinet can damage the floor. Choosing a replacement faucet when the hose is not enough

If a new hose will not solve the problem, a new faucet may. Look for a pull-down model with a solid brass body, a ceramic disc cartridge and a hose that is easy to replace. Check the hole count in your sink or countertop, since a faucet made for one hole will not fit a three-hole sink without a deck plate.

Check the height as well. A tall arc is convenient for filling pots but needs clearance under a window or cabinet. A shorter faucet fits better in a tight space. A plumber can help you match the faucet to the sink and install it with new supply lines.

Finish choices matter less than the internal parts. A faucet with a good cartridge and a solid body outlasts a showier one that uses plastic parts. Ask about warranty terms and the availability of replacement parts.

Signs the hose is the problem

A pull-down faucet that drips from the spray head, a hose that kinks or snags, or a wet cabinet floor under the sink all point to the hose or its connections. Run your hand along the underside of the line with a dry paper towel. A damp spot shows where the leak starts.

If the hose is good and the leak is at the base of the faucet, the cartridge or a seal is the next suspect. Knowing which part is leaking before you buy anything saves a second trip to the store.
