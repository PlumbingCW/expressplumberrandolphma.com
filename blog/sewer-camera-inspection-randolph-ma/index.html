Sewer Camera Inspection Results Explained | Randolph, MA

By the %s team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

A sewer camera inspection gives you a video of the inside of your drain. The picture is only useful if you can tell a normal joint from a crack and a shadow from a sag. This guide explains how to read the video and what each finding means for your house.

What a clean line looks like

On the screen, a healthy pipe looks like a round, evenly lit tunnel. The joints between pieces of pipe appear as thin lines at regular intervals, spaced by the length of a pipe section. The wall is smooth, without cracks, roots or buildup. Water at the bottom of the pipe is normal, a thin trickle during a flush.

Camera heads have lights and a self-leveling lens, so the picture stays upright as the head moves. A transmitter in the camera lets the plumber find its position above ground, so each finding comes with a depth and a location.

Footage counters

Many systems show the distance traveled in feet from the cleanout. A finding is recorded at a distance, such as 38 feet from the cleanout.

Pipe material

Clay shows short sections with visible joints. Cast iron has a rough, scaly wall. PVC is smooth and white or gray.

Which findings need action and which can wait

Not every finding needs a repair. A few fine roots at a joint can be managed with cleaning. A small offset that does not snag anything may be left alone. Collapsed pipe, a mass of roots, an active leak or a belly that holds sewage need attention.

A good plumber tells you which category each finding falls in and what could change. Our sewer camera inspection service gives you the video and a written summary, and the sewer line repair page covers the repair options.

Randolph lines and what a camera often finds

Findings that point to cleaning or repair

Cleaning is usually enough when

  • The pipe is round and intact.
  • Roots are fine and limited.
  • Grease or scale is the only problem.
  • There are no cracks or offsets.

Repair is usually needed when

  • The pipe is cracked, broken or collapsed.
  • Roots have opened a joint.
  • A belly holds waste.
  • A joint is displaced.

Next steps

Questions to ask about your video

When you review a camera video with a plumber, ask a few direct questions. How far from the cleanout is each defect? How deep is it? What is the pipe made of? Is the finding stable or likely to worsen? What are the options, and what does each involve?

Ask also whether the camera was able to travel the whole line. A video that ends at a blockage or a break does not show what lies beyond. If the line could not be completed, the plumber should say so, and explain what might be hidden.

If you are buying a house, share the video with your attorney and agent. A repair credit or a seller repair can be negotiated, but only if the findings are in writing.

What the camera cannot tell you

A camera shows the inside of the pipe, not the soil around it. It cannot say how much weight a cracked section can carry, or whether tree roots are pressing on a joint from outside. It also cannot see past a full blockage, which is why a line is usually cleaned before it is scanned.

That is why we pair the video with a plain explanation. We tell you what we saw, what we could not see and what we would do next. A scan is information to act on, not a verdict, and we would rather recommend a small repair now than a large one after a backup.
