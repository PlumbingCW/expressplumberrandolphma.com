Boiler Not Heating in Randolph, MA: What to Check First

By the %s team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

A boiler that does not heat is stressful in the middle of a New England winter. Many no-heat calls come from a few simple causes, and a few safe checks can save a service call, or at least tell the technician what to bring.

This guide covers hot water boilers, which are the most common type in Randolph homes, and tells you when to stop and call.

Five safe checks before you call

Check the thermostat

Set it several degrees above room temperature and replace the batteries if it has them.

Check the power

The boiler has a switch, often on the wall or the boiler itself, and a breaker.

Check the pressure gauge

A cold hot water boiler usually reads about 12 to 15 psi. Zero means a leak or a bad fill valve.

Check the fuel

A gas valve should be open. An oil tank should have fuel and a clean filter.

Press the reset button once

If the burner locks out again, stop and call.

If you smell gas or oil fumes, leave the house, stay out and call 911 before anything else.

Bleeding air from a radiator

Air trapped in a radiator keeps hot water from filling it, so the top stays cold.

Turn off the boiler

Let the system cool so you do not scald yourself.

Find the bleeder valve

A small valve at the top of the radiator or the end of a baseboard.

Open it slowly with a key

Hold a towel under it. Air hisses out first.

Close it when water appears

A steady stream of water means the air is out.

Check the pressure

Top up the boiler if the gauge has dropped, following the manufacturer's directions.

Work that needs a licensed professional

Massachusetts requires licensed plumbers and gas fitters for plumbing and gas work. Replacing a boiler, its relief valve or its gas piping needs a permit, and only a licensed professional can pull it. Oil burner service is handled by a licensed oil heat technician. Do not remove covers or adjust gas valves yourself.

Randolph's building department issues permits at 41 South Main Street, and the plumbing inspector is in on Mondays, Wednesdays and Thursdays. Our boiler repair page explains what we handle, and a no-heat call in cold weather is a priority on the 24/7 line.

Older Randolph homes and hot water heat

A state survey of Randolph's housing describes a town of farmhouses, side-hall cottages, Capes and bungalows. Many of these homes heat with hot water baseboard or radiators, and some larger older houses use steam. A boiler in a house of that age can be 30 years old or more.

An older boiler can still work, but its relief valve, expansion tank and circulator are wear items. Water that drips from the relief valve discharge pipe means the valve or the system pressure needs attention. We service the plumbing around the boiler, including hot water supply parts.

Hot water or steam: what to expect

Hot water system

  • Fills with water and circulates it with a pump.
  • Pressure around 12 to 15 psi when cold.
  • Radiators and baseboards fill with hot water.
  • Air in the system causes cold spots.

Steam system

  • Boils water and sends steam to radiators.
  • Water level in a sight glass matters.
  • Radiator air vents control heat.
  • A low water cutoff protects the boiler.

What to tell the dispatcher

When you call, say whether the system is hot water or steam, what fuel it uses, what the gauge reads and whether any lights or error codes show. Say whether you have any heat at all. That information tells us what parts to bring.

Preventive care that avoids no-heat calls

A boiler that gets yearly service is less likely to fail in January. The service includes checking the controls, testing the relief valve, cleaning the burner and checking the combustion. Many no-heat calls trace to a part that a service would have caught.

Between visits, keep the area around the boiler clear, watch the pressure gauge and listen for new noises. Bleed radiators at the start of the season. If you have a steam system, check the water level weekly during the heating season.

Know where your boiler switch, fuel shutoff and water shutoff are, and keep the phone number of your heating service handy. A cold house at night is easier to handle when you know where to start.

When to call for an emergency visit

Call right away if the house is dropping toward freezing, if you smell gas, if water is leaking from the boiler, or if the pressure gauge is climbing past the red zone. These are not wait-until-morning problems. A frozen pipe can turn a no-heat call into a burst-pipe call within hours.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas company from outside. Do not turn lights on or off. For a simple no-heat problem with no leak and no smell, check the thermostat, the switch and the fuel supply, then call us at (508) 389-4045 with what you found.
