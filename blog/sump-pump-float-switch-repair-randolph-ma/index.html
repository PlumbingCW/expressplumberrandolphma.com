Sump Pump Float Switch Repair in Randolph, MA: Quick Fixes

By the %s team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

When a sump pump fails, the pump itself is often fine. The float switch, the part that tells the pump when water is high enough to start, is the weak link. A float that sticks against the pit wall, tangles in a cord or wears out can leave a basement unprotected or run the pump nonstop.

This guide shows the quick checks, the fixes you can try and the point where a plumber should take over.

What a sump float switch does

A sump pump sits in a pit in the lowest part of the basement. As groundwater fills the pit, a float rises with the water. At a set height, the float tips a switch that turns the pump on. When the water drops, the float falls and the pump shuts off.

There are two common styles. A tethered float is a sealed ball on a short cord that swings free as the level rises. A vertical float rides up and down on a rod. A third type, an electronic or diaphragm switch, senses pressure and has no moving float at all. Each style fails in its own way.

Tethered float

This one can get snagged on the pit wall, the discharge pipe or its own cord. The cord can also wear through.

Vertical float

The rod can bind, or debris can wedge the float in place. It is simple and dependable in a clean pit.

Electronic switch

No moving parts, but a sensor can foul. These are often used in tight pits.

Quick checks when the pump will not start

Do these in order, and only from a dry spot.

Check power

Confirm the pump is plugged in and the outlet is live. A tripped GFCI is very common after a storm.

Look at the pit

Is there water? Is the lid in place? Is anything blocking the float?

Lift the float by hand

Lift it gently with a stick. If the pump starts, the float or its switch is the problem.

Test with water

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float rise and the pump start.

Unplug before touching

Always unplug the pump before you reach into the pit for any reason.

If the pump does not start even when the float is lifted and power is confirmed, the pump motor may have failed, and a plumber should replace it.

When a battery backup matters

A sump pump needs power at the exact time the power is most likely to fail, during a storm. A battery backup pump on its own float switch can carry the load for hours. It sits beside the main pump and runs only when the main pump cannot keep up or has no power.

A backup needs its own care. The battery should be tested and replaced on the maker's schedule, and the backup float should be set slightly higher than the main float. We install backups and test them as part of a sump pump visit.

Test your sump pump twice a year

  • Pour a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should start, empty the pit and shut off.
  • Listen for odd sounds. Grinding, rattling or a pump that runs too long needs attention.
  • Check the discharge outside. Make sure water flows away from the house and that the pipe is not frozen or blocked.
  • Check the check valve. A clapping sound after the pump stops can mean the valve is failing.
  • Test the battery backup. Unplug the main pump briefly and confirm the backup runs.

When to call

If the float is bent, the cord is frayed, the switch will not respond or the pump is more than ten years old, replacement is often the better route. A pump that fails once will likely fail again, and storm season is a bad time to find out.

Sump pump care through the seasons

In spring, test the pump before the thaw and the heavy rains. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump starts and empties it. In summer, check the discharge pipe for kinks and clear any debris from the outlet. In fall, clean the pit of silt and gravel, and make sure the discharge is clear of leaves.

In winter, the concern is the discharge. A pipe that runs out of the house and ends on the ground can freeze solid in a hard cold spell, and then the pump has nowhere to push water. A discharge that ends above ground with a loose cap or a small relief opening helps. We can check this on a visit.
