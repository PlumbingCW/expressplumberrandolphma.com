What we know about North Randolph

North Randolph is not a separately incorporated place. It is part of the town of Randolph, ZIP 02368, and the town's own new resident page refers to it when it lists the location of Fire Station 2. A public school in the area also carries the name, which shows the label is in everyday local use.

A real estate neighborhood guide describes it as a short distance from downtown, with Blue Hills Reservation just to the north and Great Pond and Powers Farm nearby. Listings there reference streets such as North Main Street, Chestnut Circle, Irving Road and Morgan Street. No official boundary line was found, so we describe the area by those anchor streets and not by a map edge.

That matters for the call. When you tell the dispatcher your street and cross street, we can place you without guessing which side of an unmarked line you are on.

The housing mix and what it means for a plumbing call

Neighborhood data sources describe a mix of single-family houses, small apartment buildings, townhouses and condominiums. Colonial Revival, Cape Cod and ranch styles are named as the most common, and one guide gives 1960 as the median year built. Another source says many homes date from 1940 to 1969, with some older.

Those are market statistics, not a plumbing inspection. A house built in 1960 may have had its pipes, water heater and drain lines replaced since, and we do not assume what is behind your wall. What the age does tell you is that it is reasonable to ask for a look at the water heater tag, the main shutoff and any visible piping early, before a small problem grows.

For condos and townhouses, one extra question comes first: is the pipe yours or the association's? Your association's documents answer that, and we can help you read what the pipe is doing before you call the manager.

If a plumbing problem hits in North Randolph

  • Water pouring from a pipe. Close the main shutoff, open a low tap to drain the lines and call. Keep clear of wet outlets and the electrical panel.
  • A drain backing up. Stop using water in the house. If several fixtures gurgle at once, the blockage is usually deeper than one trap.
  • No hot water. Check the breaker or gas valve, then call and tell us the type of water heater and its approximate age.
  • A damp basement after rain. Note where the water enters, and whether a sump pump is running. A pump that hums but does not move water needs attention soon.

Town water, sewer and permits

Water and sewer service in Randolph is run by the Department of Public Works, at 781-961-0940. The water line is 781-961-0942 and the sewer line is 781-961-0941. Randolph's water report lists hardness of 39.6 ppm, which is on the soft side, and the supply comes from the Great Pond reservoirs that Randolph shares with Holbrook.

Under the town's water regulations, the owner is responsible for the service pipe from the property line to the building. If you are not sure whether a leak is on your side or the town's, call the DPW first, then call us for the part on your side. Larger work needs a permit from the Building Department at 41 S Main St, and the plumbing inspector is in on Monday, Wednesday and Thursday.
