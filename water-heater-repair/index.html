No hot water: the usual causes we find

The same few parts account for most of these calls.

Gas: pilot or igniter

A pilot that goes out or an igniter that no longer sparks leaves a gas heater cold. The thermocouple and gas valve are the next checks.

Electric: element or thermostat

Electric tanks have two elements and two thermostats. One bad element gives lukewarm water, and two give none.

Tripped high-limit switch

A reset button on an electric tank trips when the water overheats. It trips for a reason, which a plumber finds and fixes.

Sediment and a noisy tank

Rumbling and popping mean sediment on the tank floor. A flush helps, but a very old tank may be past it.

Dip tube or mixing problem

Hot water that runs out in minutes can mean a broken dip tube or a bad tempering valve.

Leaks at the relief valve

A temperature and pressure valve that drips needs checking. The cause may be pressure, not the valve.

Gas smell or water on the floor: emergency water heater repair

Two symptoms change this from a repair call to an emergency. The first is a gas smell, which means leave the house, stay out and call 911 before anything else. The second is water at the base of a tank, which usually means the tank itself has failed.

A leaking tank is not repairable, and the call turns into a water heater replacement. A drip from a fitting, a drain valve or the relief valve discharge pipe is a different story, and those are common repair jobs.

Tank, tankless and condensing units

A conventional tank has few parts to fail, and most repairs are quick. A tankless heater is different. Error codes, scale on the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet screen or a blocked condensate drain are the common culprits, and many of these respond to a flush.

Condensing tankless units add a plastic vent and a condensate line, which can freeze or clog in a cold New England basement. Our tankless water heater service page covers flushing and repair for those.

If a repair estimate runs near the price of a new unit, we say so. Replacement is often cheaper over a few years than a second repair on an old tank.

Quick fix or new tank: how we decide

A repair is worth it when

  • The tank is sound and under ten years old.
  • A single part has failed.
  • The warranty covers the part.
  • There is no sign of leaking.

A new tank is worth it when

  • The shell leaks or the base is rusted.
  • The unit is 12 years old or more.
  • Two repairs have already been made this year.
  • The vent or gas train fails inspection.

Randolph rules for water heater work

Massachusetts requires licensed plumbers and gas fitters for water heater work. A repair that only swaps a thermostat or element is minor. Replacing a valve or piping is not, and it needs a permit and an inspection. Randolph's building department handles those permits at 41 South Main Street, and the plumbing inspector is in on Mondays, Wednesdays and Thursdays.

Because the Randolph and Holbrook water supply is soft, scale is a slower problem than in hard-water towns. A noisy tank here more often points to age and sediment than to heavy mineral buildup.

Safety steps while you wait

If the tank is leaking, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the power or gas control. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas company from outside. Do not light anything and do not flip switches.

Never block or cap the pressure relief valve, and do not touch hot pipes or a discharge that is steaming. Repairs here follow 248 CMR 10.00, the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, which sets the rules for relief valves, vents and connections.
