Emergency commercial plumbing along Route 28

Route 28 became an autohighway in the 1930s, and commercial development grew alongside it. Randolph's shops, offices and restaurants range from older mixed-use buildings near the center to newer strip frontage. Pipes, drains and water heaters in those buildings vary just as widely.

A commercial plumbing call is rarely just a plumbing call. Lost sales, spoiled product and health department rules make speed matter. We prioritize the cause, stop the water and restore the line, then recommend a permanent repair.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Find the main shutoff. Know where it is, and close it if water is flowing.
  • Protect power and stock. Move inventory and electronics off wet floors. Switch off power to wet areas.
  • Tell the property manager. Tenants in a shared building may need their own shutoff.
  • Take photos. Document the water for your insurer.

Code, inspections and backflow

Commercial plumbing in Massachusetts follows the state plumbing code, and a licensed plumber pulls the permit. Many commercial properties also have backflow assemblies that require testing, and those are separate from routine repairs.

Randolph's water regulations treat connections between drinking water and waste piping as cross-connections and set penalties for illicit connections. We advise on prevention and refer certified testing where needed.
