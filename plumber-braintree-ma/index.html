Braintree's water and what it does to pipes

Braintree runs its own water system from the Great Pond Reservoir, with the Richardi Reservoir feeding it when levels drop. Its 2024 water quality report lists hardness of 47.8 mg/L, which is soft, and a pH of 7.75. The report also shows manganese at 0.088 mg/L, above the 0.05 mg/L secondary guideline. Manganese is an aesthetic issue, and it can leave dark stains on fixtures and laundry.

For a plumber, soft water means scale builds slowly, and tank heaters tend to fail from age and corrosion. Manganese staining can clog faucet aerators and shower heads over time. If you see dark grit in your aerators, a cleaning or a cartridge replacement often restores flow.

Braintree Water and Sewer, at 781-843-8097, handles questions about the public system. Anything inside your property line, including the service pipe, is a plumber's job.

A city of mixed housing

Braintree was incorporated as a town in 1640 and became a city in 2007. Its population grew by more than half in the 1920s, and families moved in after World War II. The 2000 census counted 12,973 housing units, and the city counted 39,143 residents in 2020.

That history means a house on one street may have 1920s galvanized piping while the one next door was built in the 1950s with copper. A visit starts with finding out what is in the walls. If supply lines have been patched repeatedly, our repiping page explains the next step.
