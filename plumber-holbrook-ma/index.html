Holbrook's shared water with Randolph

Holbrook buys all its water from the Randolph-Holbrook Joint Water System, treated at the Great Pond plant in Randolph. The source is the Great Pond Reservoir System. The town's 2025 report lists a hardness of 56 ppm, in the soft range, and describes a regional treatment plant with Braintree that was scheduled to enter service in 2026.

Practically, that means Holbrook homes see the same soft water we describe on our Randolph pages. Scale builds slowly, and fixture problems are more often wear than mineral buildup.

Holbrook Public Works, at 50 North Franklin Street and 781-767-1800, handles the town system. For your own plumbing, call us.

A town built on the postwar housing boom

Holbrook was first settled in 1710 as the southern part of Old Braintree and was incorporated in 1872 as the last town carved from it. It was originally called East Randolph. After 1945, a housing boom turned it into a mostly residential town, and the 2020 census counted 4,879 housing units and 11,405 residents.

Houses from that boom commonly have copper supply lines and cast iron or clay drains. Those materials have lasted for about 70 years. They are old enough that pinhole leaks and root intrusions show up, and a sewer camera inspection is a sensible check before a major remodel or a purchase.

A note on Holbrook's Cochato River history

The Cochato River runs near Holbrook, and the EPA's Superfund cleanup of the former Baird and McGuire chemical plant concluded in 1997. It is a reminder that soil near the river is handled with care.

If you are digging for a sewer or water line repair, we follow the town's permit and notice rules and stop if the soil looks or smells unusual.
