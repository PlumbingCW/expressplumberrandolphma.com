What hydro jetting actually does

A jetting machine feeds a flexible hose with a nozzle into the pipe. Pressurized water exits through backward-facing jets that pull the hose forward and forward-facing jets that break up the clog. The wall of the pipe is washed along the whole length instead of poked once.

That matters for kitchen and main lines in older Randolph houses, where years of grease and soap scum shrink the opening. A cable through a pipe that has narrowed to a pencil width clears the clog and leaves the narrowing in place.

Pressure is set to the pipe

Older clay and cast iron pipes need lower settings than new PVC. We run a camera first to check pipe condition.

Water use

The jet uses water, and the debris is flushed down the line to the sewer or out through the cleanout.

Hydro jetting versus cabling

Cable (snake)

  • Best for a one-time hair or paper clog.
  • Cuts a path through the blockage.
  • Leaves grease and scale on the wall.
  • Lower cost per visit.

Hydro jet

  • Best for grease, scale and root hair.
  • Scours the whole circumference of the pipe.
  • Safe for sound pipe at the right pressure.
  • Higher cost, longer-lasting result.

If you are unsure which fits, start on the drain cleaning page. If the clog returns every few months, jetting is usually the right next step.

Signs a jetting visit makes sense

  • The drain clogs again within weeks. Grease and scale rebuild fast on an unscoured wall.
  • A kitchen line is slow all the time. Years of grease can leave only a thin opening.
  • Roots return after cutting. Root hair along a joint regrows, and jetting removes the fine growth.
  • A restaurant or shop grease trap line is slow. Commercial kitchen lines are a typical jetting call. See commercial plumbing for those.

When we do not jet

Jetting is the wrong tool for a collapsed, crushed or heavily cracked pipe. High pressure can worsen a damaged joint. We run a camera first when the pipe is old or the history of the line is unknown.

If the camera finds a break, we explain the sewer line repair options instead of jetting a pipe that needs replacing.

Older Randolph lines and jetting

The state survey of Randolph's housing describes a long run of postwar suburban building after 1950, when the population nearly tripled in about 25 years. Some of those houses connect to the street with cast iron or clay, and the interior of cast iron scales and rusts over decades.

A camera pass tells us whether scale is thick enough to need jetting and whether the pipe wall is sound enough to take it. The result is shown to you on the screen before and after the work.
